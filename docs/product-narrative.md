# Product Narrative

Zentrik Open CRM should be the first public demonstration that a product can be
built with its users, not merely for them.

The story is sharper than "we are releasing a free CRM." The CRM is a real
product, but it is also a live demonstration of the self-evolving product model:
users ask for things, use the product, react to releases, and Zentrik helps turn
that signal into the next product decisions.

## The Inspiring Core

The most important idea is simple:

> This CRM gets better because the people using it help build it through their
> real usage, requests, votes, complaints, and outcomes.

The product should make that loop visible. A user should be able to see:

- what people are asking for
- what the system understood from that feedback
- which ideas are being shaped
- what Codex or another agent is working on
- where human approval is required
- what shipped
- whether the release actually helped

This is the product story and the product mechanic.

The emotional center is not automation for its own sake. It is the feeling that
a useful product can become a shared living project: the community gives it
signal, Zentrik helps it understand that signal, agents prepare the work, and
humans keep the intent clean.

## Language To Use

The public language should be direct and emotionally clear.

- "built with its users" is stronger than "customer-driven."
- "grows through real usage and feedback" is stronger than "AI-powered."
- "watch the product think" captures the Buildroom better than "ideas portal."
- "signals become product work" is the simplest explanation of Zentrik's role.
- "human review" and "outcome checks" keep the autonomy believable.

The CRM should feel like a product with a heartbeat: people use it, it listens,
it learns, it changes, and it shows why.

## Why This Product

The open CRM is a concrete, public, useful product that makes the broader
Zentrik thesis easier to understand.

People can hear "self-evolving products" and still wonder what that means in
practice. A public CRM makes it visible:

- the product has real users
- those users produce signal
- signal becomes product evidence
- evidence becomes ideas and implementation work
- releases are evaluated against user outcomes
- the loop repeats

The CRM also reaches the builder community: founders, product people,
consultants, and technical operators who influence product tools, side projects,
and future teams. It gives people a way to understand Zentrik by watching a
product improve in the open.

## Mission

Build an open-source CRM that helps small teams manage account work while
showing, in public, how software can continuously improve from user engagement.

The product should help users:

- remember what matters about every account
- know what to do next
- understand which source produced each recommendation
- turn calls, emails, support notes, reviews, and usage into product signal
- use Codex safely as an account-work and product-work partner
- contribute feedback that visibly shapes the product
- see what the agent read, what it inferred, and where human judgment entered
- follow the path from request to release to outcome

## Product Principles

- **Useful first**: if the CRM is not genuinely useful, the story collapses.
- **Open by default**: users should be able to inspect, run, and improve it.
- **Local-first by default**: teams should be able to start privately.
- **Hosted when convenient**: Zentrik Open CRM Cloud should exist for teams that
  want the workflow without operating the stack.
- **Source-grounded**: recommendations should show the evidence behind them.
- **Human-centered autonomy**: agents prepare, synthesize, and implement, but
  humans approve meaningful customer-facing or product-defining work.
- **Visible evolution**: feedback, ideas, releases, and outcome checks should be
  legible from the outside.
- **Transparent agent work**: users should be able to see the context an agent
  used while it worked, then collapse that detail into an audit trail.
- **First value before connectors**: public data, starter workspaces, and manual
  signal capture should make the CRM useful before a user connects private
  systems.

## What To Omit

The story gets weaker if we overclaim. Avoid saying or implying that:

- the CRM autonomously builds anything users ask for without review
- this is just a marketing demo for Zentrik
- the product exposes private CRM data in public
- every request will ship
- AI replaces the founder, operator, seller, or product thinker

The stronger claim is more defensible: Zentrik Open CRM is built with its users
through a transparent loop where AI helps process signal, prepare work, and
accelerate improvement under human intent.

The public story should also avoid making the CRM sound disconnected from
Zentrik. The product is useful by itself, and it also shows what Zentrik can do
when a product has real signal and a visible feedback loop.

## Public Story

Use this as the short narrative:

> Zentrik Open CRM is the open-source CRM that improves with its users. Every
> request, vote, support issue, workflow pain, and usage pattern can become
> product evidence. Zentrik helps process that evidence into ideas, agent work,
> releases, and outcome checks, so the product keeps getting better in the open.

Use this more emotional version when the audience needs the mission:

> We are building a CRM people can actually use, but also a product people can
> help raise. Every useful complaint, request, vote, and workflow pattern becomes
> part of its memory. Zentrik turns that memory into better decisions, safer
> agent work, and releases users can see.

Use this as the product promise:

> A CRM for builders that remembers account context, grounds next actions in
> real sources, and lets users help shape the product they use.

Use this as the public call to action:

> Try it, self-host it, request what is missing, vote on what matters, and watch
> the product evolve.

## Intended ICP

The first ICP is not a large sales organization with a dedicated CRM admin. It
is the team that needs account discipline but rejects CRM heaviness.

Primary ICP:

- founder-led B2B teams
- indie hackers and micro-SaaS builders
- consultants and agencies managing client pipelines
- technical operators who sell, support, and build
- open-source-friendly teams that care about data control

Secondary ICP:

- product managers and CPOs who want to see Zentrik's self-evolving product
  model in a concrete public product
- teams evaluating the feedback-to-release loop in a concrete product

## Launch Narrative

The launch should connect three things:

1. This is a useful open-source CRM.
2. It was created and will continue to evolve with Zentrik.
3. Its users can directly shape what it becomes.

The public portal should not be a generic ideas board. It should feel like the
front door to the product's brain: what users are asking for, what the system is
learning, what is being shaped, and what changed because of the community.

The launch should make people want to participate, not just inspect. The ask is:
bring your real CRM pain, help us make this better than the default tools, and
watch your input become part of the product's evolution.

## Product Demonstration

The public demonstration should show:

- what signal entered the system
- what ideas Zentrik extracted or linked
- what Codex is preparing
- which parts are waiting for human validation
- what is planned for the next release
- what shipped recently
- whether the people who asked for it were satisfied
- whether usage improved after the change

This matters because it turns an abstract promise into a working artifact. The
viewer should not only understand the CRM. They should understand the operating
system behind it.
